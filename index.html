Irish Family Carers Report Crisis: 71% Struggling to Pay for Food and Heat as Isolation Deepens

<cite index="22-1,22-3">Family Carers Ireland warns that 71% of carers find it difficult to make ends meet while nearly half experience severe loneliness</cite>, according to the State of Caring 2026 report.

Almost half of family carers in Ireland — 48% — have been diagnosed with a mental health condition. Not burned out. Not stressed. Diagnosed. That number sits differently when you say it out loud, because behind it are hundreds of thousands of people who got up every single day, took care of someone they love, and quietly fell apart in the process. If that's you, or someone you know, this article is for you.

The hours alone tell part of the story. The average Irish carer provides 38.7 hours of care per week — a full-time job, unpaid, often invisible. Nearly a third (29%) are doing 43 hours or more, up from 21% in 2016. That kind of sustained pressure doesn't just wear you down physically — it rewires how you feel about yourself, your future, and whether you're allowed to need anything at all.

The mental health figures are stark. 35% of Irish family carers have been diagnosed with depression. 39% with anxiety. And yet 72% have never accessed respite care — not once. There's a particular cruelty in that gap: the people who most need a break are the least likely to have taken one, often because there's no one to step in, or because stepping away feels like letting someone down.

What makes this harder is how alone carers tend to feel in it. There's no HR department, no sick days, no performance review that tells you you're doing a good job. The mental toll of caring is real, it is common, and it deserves to be talked about openly — not tucked away as a private struggle. You are not weak for finding this hard. You are human, doing an extraordinary thing under extraordinary pressure.

If you're a carer who's been running on empty, please know that support exists — even if it doesn't always feel that way. Talking to your GP is a good first step. So is reaching out to Family Carers Ireland, who offer free counselling and peer support. And if the day-to-day coordination of care is adding to your load — medication reminders, tracking appointments, keeping the family in the loop — RonCare was built specifically for families in this situation. It won't fix everything, but it can take some of the weight off your shoulders. You can find out more at roncare.ie.
