Ireland Increases Carer's Benefit Income Disregard to €1,000 Weekly from July 2026

Government raises income thresholds allowing family carers to earn more while retaining social protection benefits, addressing longstanding financial pressure on unpaid carers.

There are now more than 624,000 family carers in Ireland — people who get up every morning and quietly hold the world together for someone they love. A new report from Family Carers Ireland found that 71% are finding it hard to make ends meet, and almost half experience severe loneliness. This week, two things have happened that every carer in Ireland should know about: the income rules for Carer's Allowance changed significantly on 2 July 2026, and a landmark law — the Health (Amendment) (Home Support Providers) Act 2026 — was signed, meaning home care in Ireland will be regulated and inspected by HIQA for the very first time.

If you care for a parent, partner, or child full-time, Carer's Allowance is the main payment to look at. The current rate is €270 per week if you are under 66, or €308 per week if you are 66 or older. From 2 July 2026, Minister Dara Calleary introduced a major increase to the income disregard — you can now earn up to €1,000 per week (or €2,000 as a couple) and still qualify in full. On savings: only the amount above €20,000 is assessed, at a rate of €1 per week for every €1,000 over that threshold.

If you were turned down before, apply again now — almost 2,700 existing carers received a higher payment from that date alone. You can apply through MyWelfare at mywelfare.ie, or ring Citizens Information on 0761 07 4000 for a plain-English walkthrough before you fill in a form.

Alongside Carer's Allowance, there is the Carer's Support Grant — €2,000 paid every June, one payment per person you care for, and it is not means-tested. Over 147,000 carers received it in June 2026. If you are already on Carer's Allowance, Carer's Benefit, or Domiciliary Care Allowance, the grant lands in your account automatically — no form needed.

If you are not on any of those payments but you are providing full-time care and not working more than 18.5 hours a week outside the home, you can still claim it using form CSG1 from the Department of Social Protection. The grant is fully tax-free and you can spend it on whatever you need — a short break, home adaptations, a new piece of equipment, or simply breathing room.

If the person you care for needs help at home with washing, dressing, or meals, ask your GP or Public Health Nurse about the HSE Home Support Service. It is needs-assessed, not means-tested, so your income does not affect eligibility. Priority goes to people leaving hospital, those at risk of moving to a nursing home, and situations where the family carer needs a break. To apply, download the form from hse.ie/homesupportservice or call HSE Live free on 1800 700 700, Monday to Friday 8am–8pm and Saturday 9am–5pm. The new Home Support Providers Act means every agency delivering that care will soon have to meet national standards set by HIQA — a real change for families who want to know the person coming through their door meets a proper baseline.

If you do one thing today, call Citizens Information on 0761 07 4000 — they will tell you, in plain English, exactly what you are entitled to right now. You'd be surprised how many carers are sitting on payments they've never claimed, simply because nobody told them to look. You are not alone in this, even on the hard days.
