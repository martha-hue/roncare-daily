The RonCare Spotlight

Real facts, real people — shining a light on the hidden world of family caring in Ireland

Nearly half of all family carers in Ireland — 48% — have been diagnosed with a mental health condition. Not burned out. Not stressed. Diagnosed. That means hundreds of thousands of people across this country are quietly carrying not just the weight of someone else's care, but the weight of their own unravelling mental health, often without anyone asking how they're doing.

The numbers are stark. When you're providing an average of 38.7 hours of care every single week, with no pay and often no backup, your own health becomes the thing that gets put last. 44% of carers in Ireland experience physical or emotional harm as a direct result of their caring role. That's not a personal failing. That's a system that asks too much of people and gives back too little.

What makes this harder is the silence around it. Caring can feel like something you're just supposed to do — for a parent, a partner, a child. Admitting that it's breaking you can feel like a betrayal of the person you love. But you're not alone in this, and struggling doesn't mean you're failing.

The good news is that support does exist. Carer's Allowance, the €2,000 Carer's Support Grant, GP Visit Cards, and respite options through the HSE are real things available right now that carers are entitled to. The first step is knowing they exist, and knowing that asking for help isn't giving up — it's how you keep going.

If you're caring for someone at home and you're struggling, you don't have to figure it out alone. RonCare (roncare.ie) was built specifically for families in exactly this situation — to help you coordinate care, stay on top of the practical side, and feel a little less like you're carrying it all by yourself.
