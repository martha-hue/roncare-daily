State of Caring 2026 Report Exposes Crisis: 71% of Irish Family Carers Struggle to Make Ends Meet

Nearly half of Ireland's family carers are cutting back on food and heating while 75% have never accessed formal respite care support.

There are an estimated 624,000 family carers in Ireland right now — people quietly getting on with it, looking after a parent, a child, a partner, often with little financial help and even less recognition. A major new report from Family Carers Ireland published in June 2026 put the reality into sharp focus: 71% of carers are struggling to make ends meet, and almost half experience severe loneliness. The good news is that real changes to carer supports have just come into effect, and many Irish carers haven't yet claimed everything they're entitled to.

The biggest recent change is to Carer's Allowance — the weekly payment from the Department of Social Protection for people who have left work or cut their hours to care full-time for a loved one. Since 2 July 2026, the income disregard in the means test jumped from €625 to €1,000 for a single carer, and from €1,250 to €2,000 for a couple — the largest increase in the scheme's history. In practice, this means a two-adult household earning up to €110,000 a year can still qualify for the full payment.

The current rate is €270 a week if you're under 66, or €308 a week if you're 66 or older. If you're caring for two or more people, you can receive 50% on top of that — up to €405 or €462 a week. If you were refused before or got a reduced payment, it's worth applying again now.

Alongside Carer's Allowance, there's also the Carer's Support Grant — a €2,000 annual lump sum paid every June. It is not means-tested, fully tax-free, and you get one payment for every person you care for. If you're already on Carer's Allowance, Carer's Benefit, or Domiciliary Care Allowance, you receive it automatically. If you're not on any of those payments but you provide full-time care and haven't worked outside the home for more than 18.5 hours a week, you can still apply — the form is CSG1 from the Department of Social Protection.

If your loved one needs help at home beyond what you can give, the HSE Home Support Service is free and not means-tested — you don't even need a medical card. You can apply directly by contacting your local HSE Home Support Office, or by calling HSE Live on 1800 700 700 (Monday to Friday, 8am to 8pm).

A question many carers ask quietly is whether a parent can pay them for the care they provide. The answer is yes — there's no rule preventing a family arrangement where your mum or dad pays you from their own income or savings. However, any income you receive will be assessed in the Carer's Allowance means test, so it's worth getting advice from Citizens Information first.

You can call Citizens Information on 0761 07 4000, or pop into your nearest Citizens Information Centre — they can walk through the numbers with you and flag any other supports you might be missing, including the Household Benefits Package, Free Travel, and Fuel Allowance, which Carer's Allowance recipients may also be able to access.

If you're caring for someone today and you're not sure what you're entitled to, ring Citizens Information on 0761 07 4000. They won't judge, they won't rush you, and they know exactly which payments and services apply to your situation. You give so much — make sure you're getting back everything you're owed.
