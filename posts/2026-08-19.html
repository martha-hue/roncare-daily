The Population Ageing Crisis

The growing pressure on health and social care systems as Europe's population ages

Something significant is shifting across Europe right now, and if you have a parent, grandparent, or someone you love who needs care, it touches you directly. The European Union is facing a historic demographic turning point — population decline is projected to begin soon, driven by an ageing society that health and social care systems were simply not built for. By 2050, three in ten people in the EU will be over 65, up from two in ten today, and the share of those aged 85 and over will more than double. Governments from Dublin to London are finally being forced to act — but the question is whether they can move fast enough.

In England, the pressure on social care has become impossible to ignore. A&E attendances by over-65s jumped from 3.3 million in 2011/12 to 5.8 million in 2024/25 — a stark sign of how badly the system is struggling. The government has launched the 'Big Conversation on Care', an open online platform where carers, families, and older people can share what they need from the system.

England's adult social care workforce is in crisis. A July 2026 report from the think tank Re:State found that vacancy rates stood at 6.8% in 2024–25, equivalent to around 111,000 unfilled posts, with staff turnover at 23.7%. Of workers considering leaving, 67% cite the toll on their health and wellbeing as a key reason, yet only around 16% have ever accessed therapy services. The Health Foundation estimates that a Scottish-style model of free personal care for over-65s in England would cost around £6.5 billion extra in 2026/27, rising to £7.5 billion by 2035/36.

Ireland is facing its own reckoning. On 1 July 2026, Ireland enacted the Health (Amendment) (Home Support Providers) Act 2026, which creates a legal route to register and inspect home care providers through HIQA — long overdue. Minister for Older People Kieran O'Donnell secured €92 million in additional Fair Deal funding for nursing homes in Budget 2026, plus 1.7 million extra home support hours, bringing the national total to 26.7 million hours. But the Home Care Coalition — representing 23 charities and advocacy groups — points out that state funding still prioritises residential care (€1.23bn) over home support (€838m), and that no legal right to home care exists in Ireland yet.

For families and carers, these changes are not abstract policy debates — they are the difference between getting help at home or watching a loved one wait months on a list. The political momentum in both the UK and Ireland is stronger than it has been in years. What matters now is that governments back their words with funded plans. Dignity and independence in older age should never feel like a lottery.
