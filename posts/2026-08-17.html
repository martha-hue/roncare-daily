Today's Briefing

Cleveland Clinic Demonstrates Scale of Ambient AI Scribe Deployment Across Enterprise

Health systems achieving sustained ambient scribe adoption through governance, phased onboarding and continuous feedback mechanisms.

Something big is shifting in healthcare right now — and if you've ever sat on hold trying to book a doctor's appointment, or watched a nurse spend precious minutes buried in paperwork instead of talking to patients, you'll want to hear this. In the past week alone, major announcements have landed that bring AI directly into the everyday business of finding care, coordinating appointments, and keeping the administrative wheels turning. These aren't distant experiments. They're live, affecting real patients and real staff today.

The headline story this week is the new partnership between Google Gemini and Zocdoc, announced on August 12, 2026. Gemini users in the US can now find and book appointments through Zocdoc's network of over 200,000 providers across more than 200 specialties and 10,000 insurance plans — all without calling anyone or hunting through directories.

On the clinical administration side, athenahealth this week announced the broad availability of AI capabilities across its athenaOne network and launched the EHR AI CoLab — a co-innovation forum where clinicians and superusers help shape new tools before they go live. Its ambient documentation tool, athenaAmbient, listens during patient visits and automatically drafts clinical notes, diagnoses, and prescriptions, freeing doctors from the keyboard so they can stay focused on the person in front of them.

There's also a stubborn problem in the background that AI is beginning to crack open: prior authorizations. A fresh Inovalon survey found that 93% of healthcare organisations still rely on phone calls, fax machines, or manual payer portals to manage prior authorisations — a process that delays care and frustrates everyone involved.

Taken together, this week's news shows AI quietly moving from the hospital server room into the daily lives of patients and the people who care for them. Booking appointments is getting easier, paperwork is getting lighter, and the people who look after your loved ones are getting a little more breathing room. The technology isn't perfect, and how each tool is governed still matters enormously — but the direction of travel is clear. AI in healthcare admin is no longer coming. It's here.
