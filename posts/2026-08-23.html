The RonCare Spotlight

Real facts, real people — shining a light on the hidden world of family caring in Ireland

Almost half of all family carers in Ireland — 48% — have been diagnosed with a mental health condition. That's not a footnote. That's nearly one in two people who quietly took on the role of looking after someone they love, and paid for it with their own wellbeing. Depression affects 35% of carers, anxiety 39%, and yet most people still think of caring as something you just get on with. You don't talk about it. You don't admit it's breaking you. But the numbers say it is.

The hours alone tell a story. The average Irish family carer gives 38.7 hours of care every week — a full working week, unpaid, often on top of an actual job or raising children. Nearly a third of carers (29%) are doing 43 hours or more, up from 21% in 2016. That kind of sustained pressure doesn't just wear you down emotionally — it grinds you down physically too.

Forty percent of carers have a back injury from caring, and 67% have been diagnosed or treated for a physical health condition. What makes it harder is the isolation. Caring tends to shrink your world — social plans get cancelled, friendships drift, and the person you used to be starts to feel very far away.

When there's no break in sight, that weight becomes something else entirely. Seventy-two percent of Irish family carers have never accessed respite care — never had a week, or even a weekend, where someone else took over. And 44% have experienced physical or emotional harm as a direct result of their caring role. These aren't statistics about weakness. They're statistics about a system that leaves people to carry too much, alone.

If you're struggling, that matters — and it's worth saying plainly: you are not failing your loved one by admitting it. Organisations like Family Carers Ireland offer free support lines, peer groups, and counselling specifically for carers. Your GP is also a starting point — not just for physical symptoms, but for referrals to mental health supports you may not know you're entitled to. You are allowed to ask for help. In fact, asking for help is one of the most important things you can do — for yourself and for the person you care for.

If you're managing care at home, RonCare was built with you in mind. It's a care coordination app designed to help Irish families stay organised, share the load, and feel a little less alone in all of it — whether that's tracking medications, logging care notes, or simply keeping everyone in the loop. It won't fix the system, but it can take a few things off your plate. You can find out more at roncare.ie.
